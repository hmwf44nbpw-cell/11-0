How it works

Each draw is a country and a decade that has at least one player who fits a gap in your side. Choose a player, then choose where he bats. Batters and keepers can only bat in a position where they played at least 40% of their Test innings (No. 1 and No. 2 count together as opening). All-rounders can bat anywhere, and bowlers bat from No. 8 to 11. Once your XI is complete, the bowlers are sorted into batting order by career batting average. Ratings stay hidden until the XI is complete, so judge on the career numbers.

Building the side

Six batters, a wicketkeeper, an all-rounder and three bowlers of any type. Extra all-rounders can fill batting spots. If you pick a batter, you choose where in the order he goes, within his usual positions. Your finished XI then plays an 11-Test series away from home, each against a different country and decade, simulated from the players' career numbers.

The numbers

Career Test averages, wickets, strike rates and batting positions come from ESPNcricinfo Statsguru, current to November 2026. Balls faced weren't recorded for most Tests before the mid-1970s, so batting strike rates for many older players are estimates. A player appears under each decade he played a meaningful part of his career in. Ratings and bowling styles are our own judgment.
